# MOSAIC Experimental Design and Results

## Mission-Oriented Self-Organization through Auctions, Incentives, and Coalitions

**Version:** 0.4.0  
**Authors:** Katherin Molina and Lorena Holguin  
**Model implementation:** NetLogo 7.0.4  
**Official experimental scope:** BS01–BS07  
**Total runs:** 690  
**Random seeds:** 1–30, paired across conditions  
**Document status:** Final experimental-design and results record for the v0.4.0 release

---

## 1. Purpose of this document

This document records the complete experimental design and empirical output of
the MOSAIC v0.4.0 simulation program.

It has four purposes:

1. define the official BehaviorSpace experiments;
2. document the common baseline and paired-seed design;
3. report the statistical findings from BS01–BS07;
4. distinguish supported conclusions from descriptive patterns and
   non-significant effects.

The model specification is documented separately in
`MOSAIC_ODD_Protocol_v0.4.0.md`. Operational instructions are documented in
`MOSAIC_User_Manual_v0.4.0.md`.

This document reports simulation evidence. It does not claim field validation
or direct predictive accuracy for a physical robot mission.

---

## 2. Research objective

MOSAIC investigates whether incentive mechanisms can transform decentralized
competition among heterogeneous robots into self-organized mission coordination
without central supervision.

The experimental program evaluates six related mechanisms:

- reward allocation;
- communication reach and reliability;
- capability heterogeneity;
- cooperative-task demand;
- reputation and adaptive strategies;
- mission-incentive strength.

The analysis focuses on a distinction between:

- **mission outcomes**, such as completion and task value; and
- **coordination processes**, such as bidding, coalition formation,
  communication, release, inequality, and overhead.

This distinction is necessary because a model can achieve high completion while
using a costly or congested coordination process.

### 3.2 BehaviorSpace execution

Every official experiment uses:

```text
setup command: setup
go command: go
repetitions: 1
random seeds: 1–30
run metrics every step: false
update view: false
time limit: 500 ticks
```

The terminal condition is:

```netlogo
not any? tasks with [not completed? and not failed?]
or ticks >= mission-time-limit
or not any? robots with [energy > 0]
```

### 3.3 Paired-seed design

The same 30 random seeds are reused across conditions within each experiment.

This design reduces unexplained stochastic variation because each condition is
evaluated against comparable random environments, robot populations, and task
configurations generated by the same seed.

The analysis therefore uses paired or repeated-measures methods rather than
treating conditions as independent samples.

- explored area;

- energy use.

### 4.2 Auction and coalition outcomes

- total bids;
- contested auction rounds;
- unresolved auction rounds;
- contracts and memberships awarded;
- coalition-formation attempts;
- coalitions formed;
- coalition success;
- coalition formation efficiency;
- formation delay;
- contract releases.

### 4.3 Information and network outcomes

- communication links;
- mean network degree;
- network density;
- isolated robots;
- information transmissions;
- unique acquisitions;
- information coverage;
- information redundancy.

### 4.4 Economic and distributional outcomes

- rewards distributed;
- mission bonuses;
- Reward Gini;
- Contract Gini;
- top-reward share.

### 4.5 Adaptation outcomes

- mean, minimum, and maximum reputation;
- strategy composition;
- strategy switches.

### 4.6 Composite indices

```text
coordination overhead =
(bids + transmissions + coalition attempts + releases)
divided by completed tasks
```

```text
mission coordination efficiency =
task value
divided by
(energy used + bids + unique acquisitions + coalition attempts + releases)
```

These are comparative model indices. They are not calibrated physical units.

## 5. Statistical approach

### 5.1 Descriptive statistics

For every condition, the analysis reports:

- mean;
- median where relevant;
- standard deviation;
- minimum and maximum;
- run counts;
- failure decomposition.

### 5.2 Repeated-measures tests

Experiments with three or four repeated conditions use the Friedman test for
overall differences.

When the omnibus test is significant, paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests are
used for condition comparisons.

### 5.3 Factorial tests

BS03 and BS06 use paired factorial contrasts.

BS03 estimates:

- communication-radius main effect;
- communication-failure main effect;
- radius × failure interaction.

BS06 estimates:

- reputation main effect;
- adaptation main effect;
- reputation × adaptation interaction.

### 5.4 Multiple-comparison control

Holm correction is used for families of planned pairwise or multi-outcome
comparisons.

A corrected probability below 0.05 is treated as statistically significant.

### 5.5 Interpretation rule

A descriptive difference is not presented as a supported effect unless it is
consistent across paired seeds and passes the relevant statistical test.

# 6. BS01 — Baseline Replication

## 6.1 Purpose

BS01 estimates the stochastic performance of the validated baseline and
provides the control condition reproduced inside later experiments.

### 6.2 Design

```text
conditions = 1
seeds = 30
runs = 30
```

### 6.3 Results

| Metric | Result |
|---|---:|
| Mean completion rate | 92.22% |
| Completion-rate standard deviation | 6.80 percentage points |
| Mean completed tasks | 27.67 of 30 |
| Mean failed tasks | 2.33 |
| Full-completion runs | 4 of 30 |
| Runs at or above 90% | 23 of 30 |
| Mean information coverage | 95.16% |
| Mean coalition success | 78.24% |
| Mean coalition formation efficiency | 4.79% |
| Mean coalition delay | 42.68 ticks |
| Mean coordination overhead | 75.40 |
| Mean mission efficiency | 1.482 |
| Mean Reward Gini | 0.296 |
| Mean Contract Gini | 0.259 |
| Mean top-reward share | 36.01% |

### 6.4 Failure decomposition

| Failure category | Count | Share |
|---|---:|---:|
| Undiscovered | 28 | 40.00% |
| No feasible allocation | 38 | 54.29% |
| Deadline after assignment | 0 | 0.00% |
| Other | 4 | 5.71% |

### 6.5 Interpretation

The baseline is high-performing but not trivial. Mean completion exceeds 90%,
yet the standard deviation and lower-performing seeds show that mission success
is sensitive to stochastic spatial and capability configurations.

The dominant failure mechanism is not post-assignment execution. It is the
inability to produce a feasible allocation before task expiration.

This baseline is therefore suitable for mechanism comparison because it leaves
observable variation in completion, coalition formation, overhead, and
distribution without collapsing into either universal success or universal
failure.

# 7. BS02 — Reward Regime Comparison

## 7.1 Research question

Do different reward-allocation rules change mission performance, coalition
outcomes, coordination friction, or inequality?

### 7.2 Conditions

- `leader-priority`
- `equal-share`
- `contribution-based`
- `mission-aligned`

Each condition uses the same 30 seeds.

### 7.3 Hypotheses

1. Reward rules may change completion by altering expected utility.
2. Leader-priority may increase concentration and bidding competition.
3. Contribution-based rewards may reduce inequality.
4. Mission-aligned rewards may improve cooperation at an additional economic
   cost.

### 7.4 Condition means

| Reward regime | Completion | Coalition success | Unresolved rounds | Formation efficiency | Reward Gini | Contract Gini |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `leader-priority` | 92.11% | 75.70% | 568.43 | 3.58% | 0.339 | 0.265 |
| `equal-share` | 92.22% | 78.24% | 338.80 | 4.79% | 0.296 | 0.259 |
| `contribution-based` | 92.11% | 80.09% | 366.50 | 4.35% | 0.289 | 0.259 |
| `mission-aligned` | 91.11% | 82.00% | 336.83 | 4.57% | 0.300 | 0.261 |

### 7.5 Statistical results

Completion did not differ significantly across regimes:

```text
Friedman p = 0.8022
```

Coalition success also did not show a robust global difference:

```text
Friedman p = 0.1284
```

Unresolved auction rounds differed strongly:

```text
Friedman p = 0.0000
```

Reward inequality also differed:

```text
Friedman p = 0.0301
```

Leader-priority produced approximately:

```text
229.63 more unresolved rounds
than equal-share.
```

Its Reward Gini was approximately:

```text
0.050 higher
than contribution-based.
```

### 7.6 Interpretation

Reward allocation changed process and distribution more than final completion.

Leader-priority did not improve the mission. It generated greater auction
friction and higher reward concentration. Contribution-based and equal-share
payments produced fairer distributions without a completion penalty.

The evidence does not support the proposition that stronger concentration of
rewards produces superior decentralized coordination in this model.

### 7.7 Conclusion

**Completion hypothesis:** not supported.  
**Leader-priority friction hypothesis:** supported.  
**Distributional hypothesis:** supported.  
**Recommended baseline regime:** `equal-share`.

# 8. BS03 — Communication Degradation

## 8.1 Research question

Is mission performance more sensitive to structural communication reach or to a
moderate probability of temporary link failure?

### 8.2 Factorial design

```text
communication-radius = 4 or 8
communication-failure-rate = 0.05 or 0.35
```

This creates four paired conditions and 120 runs.

### 8.3 Condition means

| Condition | Completion | Information coverage | Isolated robots | Coalition delay | Coordination overhead | Mission efficiency |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Short range / reliable | 89.00% | 87.86% | 10.83 | 79.03 | 103.31 | 1.050 |
| Short range / unreliable | 88.78% | 88.11% | 12.17 | 79.88 | 104.85 | 1.016 |
| Wide range / reliable | 92.22% | 95.16% | 4.60 | 42.68 | 75.40 | 1.482 |
| Wide range / unreliable | 92.67% | 95.53% | 6.33 | 50.63 | 88.53 | 1.249 |

### 8.4 Radius effects

Increasing radius from 4 to 8 changed:

| Outcome | Paired mean effect | p-value |
|---|---:|---:|
| Completion rate | 3.56 percentage points | 0.0002 |
| Information coverage | 7.36 percentage points | 0.0000 |
| Isolated robots | -6.03 | 0.0000 |
| Coalition delay | -32.80 ticks | 0.0000 |
| Coordination overhead | -22.12 | 0.0000 |
| Mission efficiency | 0.332 | 0.0000 |

Wide communication increased completion and information coverage while reducing
isolation, formation delay, and coordination overhead.

### 8.5 Failure-rate effects

Increasing link failure from 0.05 to 0.35 changed completion by only:

```text
0.11 percentage points
p = 0.8925
```

It increased final isolation by:

```text
1.53 robots
p = 0.0006
```

The link-failure intervention altered network state but did not produce a
reliable completion decline.

### 8.6 Interpretation

Structural communication reach is more consequential than moderate temporary
link unreliability.

Repeated opportunities for network reconstruction and knowledge exchange allow
the system to compensate for many temporary failures. A short communication
radius, however, persistently restricts partner access and information
diffusion.

### 8.7 Conclusion

**Communication-radius hypothesis:** supported.  
**Moderate failure-rate completion hypothesis:** not supported.  
**Design implication:** preserve reach or introduce relay mechanisms before
focusing on moderate per-link reliability improvements.

# 9. BS04 — Capability Heterogeneity

## 9.1 Research question

Does capability heterogeneity improve mission performance through
complementarity, or reduce performance through matching complexity?

### 9.2 Conditions

```text
capability-heterogeneity = 0.0, 0.5, 1.0
```

### 9.3 Condition means

| Condition | Completion | Failed tasks | Formation efficiency | Coalition success | Unresolved rounds | Reward Gini |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Homogeneous (`0.0`) | 86.56% | 4.03 | 2.48% | 86.27% | 446.53 | 0.300 |
| Moderate (`0.5`) | 92.22% | 2.33 | 4.79% | 78.24% | 338.80 | 0.296 |
| High (`1.0`) | 86.00% | 4.20 | 3.25% | 73.36% | 480.47 | 0.356 |

### 9.4 Statistical results

The overall completion difference was significant:

```text
Friedman p = 0.0021
```

Holm-adjusted paired comparisons:

| Comparison | Adjusted p-value | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---|
| 0.0 versus 0.5 | 0.0017 | Moderate heterogeneity performed better. |
| 0.0 versus 1.0 | 0.7269 | No reliable difference between extremes. |
| 0.5 versus 1.0 | 0.0010 | Moderate heterogeneity performed better. |

### 9.5 Interpretation

The relationship is non-monotonic.

Homogeneous teams lack enough complementary variation. Highly heterogeneous
teams contain strong specialization but create greater matching difficulty,
longer delays, and more unequal outcomes.

Moderate heterogeneity balances these effects. It provides enough capability
diversity to construct feasible teams without making partner matching
excessively difficult.

### 9.6 Conclusion

**Intermediate-optimum hypothesis:** supported.

The tested optimum is:

```text
capability-heterogeneity = 0.5
```

This is a model-specific result, not a universal physical design constant.

# 10. BS05 — Cooperative Task Demand

## 10.1 Research question

How much cooperative-task demand can the decentralized coalition mechanism
absorb before mission performance deteriorates nonlinearly?

### 10.2 Conditions

```text
coalition-task-proportion = 0.0, 0.4, 0.8
```

### 10.3 Condition means

| Condition | Completion | Cooperative tasks | Completed cooperative tasks | Formation efficiency | Unresolved rounds | Coordination overhead | Mission efficiency |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| No cooperative demand (`0.0`) | 98.89% | 0.00 | 0.00 | 0.00% | 0.00 | 14.84 | 3.314 |
| Baseline demand (`0.4`) | 92.22% | 12.90 | 11.20 | 4.79% | 338.80 | 75.40 | 1.482 |
| High demand (`0.8`) | 75.67% | 24.23 | 17.43 | 3.51% | 649.63 | 140.40 | 0.919 |

### 10.4 Statistical results

Completion differed strongly:

```text
Friedman p = 0.000000
```

The declines were:

```text
0.0 to 0.4: -6.67 percentage points
0.4 to 0.8: -16.56 percentage points
```

The second decline was significantly larger:

```text
second-difference Wilcoxon p = 0.0025
```

At high demand:

```text
170 of 219 failures
(77.63%)
were caused by no feasible allocation.
```

### 10.5 Interpretation

The model exhibits a cooperative-demand threshold.

The issue is not primarily that formed coalitions fail during execution.
Coalition success remains comparatively strong. The main constraint is the
capacity to find, form, and synchronize enough feasible coalitions before
deadlines.

High cooperative demand increases:

- bid volume;
- coalition attempts;
- unresolved auctions;
- formation delay;
- coordination overhead;
- allocation failures.

### 10.6 Conclusion

**Demand-overload hypothesis:** supported.  
**Nonlinear-threshold hypothesis:** supported.  
**Principal development priority:** coalition discovery and matching.

# 11. BS06 — Reputation × Adaptation

## 11.1 Research question

Do reputation and adaptive strategies improve mission performance, either
independently or through interaction?

### 11.2 Factorial design

```text
reputation-enabled? = false or true
adaptive-strategies? = false or true
```

### 11.3 Condition means

| Condition | Completion | Coalition success | Formation efficiency | Coalition delay | Contract releases | Strategy switches | Mission efficiency |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Neither mechanism | 91.00% | 77.51% | 4.53% | 44.44 | 3.40 | 0.00 | 1.344 |
| Reputation only | 90.89% | 75.27% | 4.22% | 49.04 | 4.00 | 0.00 | 1.221 |
| Adaptation only | 91.56% | 77.38% | 4.28% | 47.72 | 3.43 | 52.63 | 1.255 |
| Reputation + adaptation | 92.22% | 78.24% | 4.79% | 42.68 | 3.50 | 52.23 | 1.482 |

### 11.4 Main effects

Reputation main effect on completion:

```text
0.28 percentage points
p = 0.7807
```

Adaptation main effect on completion:

```text
0.94 percentage points
p = 0.2909
```

Neither effect was significant.

When adaptation was enabled, the model generated:

```text
52.43 strategy switches per run on average.
```

This confirms that the mechanism was active, even though it did not produce a
reliable completion benefit.

### 11.5 Interaction

The reputation × adaptation interaction for coalition delay was:

```text
-9.65 ticks
Holm-adjusted p = 0.0307
```

The combined condition shortened formation delay beyond a purely additive
expectation.

The interaction did not produce a corresponding significant completion gain.

### 11.6 Interpretation

Reputation and adaptation are not universal baseline improvements.

The baseline mission may be too short, too forgiving, or insufficiently
differentiated for reliability learning to produce strong selection value.
Similarly, frequent strategy switching can create behavioral activity without
creating better mission outcomes.

The significant delay interaction suggests that the mechanisms may improve a
coordination process even when they do not change final completion.

### 11.7 Conclusion

**Reputation main-effect hypothesis:** not supported.  
**Adaptation main-effect hypothesis:** not supported.  
**Joint process-value hypothesis:** partially supported.  
**Future use:** test under longer, harsher, or reliability-differentiated
missions.

# 12. BS07 — Mission Incentive Strength

## 12.1 Research question

Does increasing mission-aligned incentive strength improve cooperation and
mission performance enough to justify the additional reward cost?

### 12.2 Conditions

```text
reward-regime = "mission-aligned"
mission-incentive-weight = 0.00, 0.10, 0.25, 0.50
```

### 12.3 Condition means

| Incentive weight | Completion | Coalition success | Contract releases | Mission efficiency | Rewards distributed | Reward Gini |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `0.00` | 92.22% | 78.24% | 3.50 | 1.482 | 3337.58 | 0.296 |
| `0.10` | 92.67% | 81.94% | 2.80 | 1.508 | 3680.33 | 0.303 |
| `0.25` | 91.11% | 82.00% | 2.80 | 1.459 | 4096.76 | 0.300 |
| `0.50` | 91.56% | 82.68% | 2.63 | 1.465 | 4970.47 | 0.317 |

### 12.4 Statistical results

Completion did not differ significantly:

```text
Friedman p = 0.2109
```

Coalition success did not differ significantly:

```text
Friedman p = 0.1200
```

Reward Gini did not show a robust difference:

```text
Friedman p = 0.6334
```

Contract Gini produced an omnibus signal:

```text
Friedman p = 0.0293
```

but no individual pair remained significant after Holm correction.

The high-incentive condition increased mean rewards by:

```text
48.92%
```

relative to zero bonus, without a significant completion improvement.

### 12.5 Interpretation

The model exhibits incentive saturation.

A low incentive produced the best descriptive completion, but the advantage
was small and inconsistent across paired seeds. Moderate and high incentives
increased payouts substantially while producing lower mean completion than the
zero-bonus condition.

The economic mechanism changed payment volume more strongly than mission
behavior.

### 12.6 Conclusion

**Completion-improvement hypothesis:** not supported.  
**Coalition-success hypothesis:** not supported.  
**Cost-growth hypothesis:** supported.  
**Recommended baseline mission incentive:** zero; 0.10 only as a low-cost
experimental alternative.

# 13. Integrated findings

## 13.1 Coordination capacity is the dominant constraint

Across the experimental program, formed coalitions often succeeded. The most
persistent failures arose earlier:

- task information did not reach enough robots;
- no individually feasible bid existed;
- no feasible coalition was found;
- auction rounds remained unresolved;
- formation consumed too much time before deadlines.

This means that execution quality and coordination capacity are different
problems.

The current principal bottleneck is coalition discovery and matching.

## 13.2 Structural conditions matter more than stronger payments

Communication radius, capability composition, and cooperative demand produced
larger and more reliable completion effects than reward regime or incentive
weight.

The evidence supports the following ordering of practical importance under the
tested baseline:

1. manageable cooperative demand;
2. sufficient communication reach;
3. moderate capability heterogeneity;
4. coalition-matching efficiency;
5. reward-distribution design;
6. reputation, adaptation, and incentive strength.

This is not a general theoretical ranking. It is the ranking produced by the
implemented mechanisms and parameter ranges.

## 13.3 Heterogeneity has an intermediate optimum

Heterogeneity creates two opposing forces:

- complementary capabilities increase the probability that aggregate
  requirements can be met;
- specialization increases the search and matching burden.

The observed optimum at 0.5 demonstrates that “more heterogeneity” is not
equivalent to “better coordination.”

## 13.4 Communication reach is a structural resource

A wider radius improves:

- information coverage;
- partner access;
- network connectivity;
- coalition formation delay;
- completion;
- coordination overhead.

Moderate link failure did not remove these benefits because the network is
reconstructed repeatedly.

## 13.5 Cooperative demand exposes a nonlinear capacity limit

The decline from 0.4 to 0.8 cooperative-task proportion was much larger than
the decline from 0.0 to 0.4.

The model therefore does not respond linearly to additional cooperative demand.
Once coalition formation becomes the dominant workload, unresolved matching
grows faster than successful coordination capacity.

## 13.6 Reward design affects fairness and congestion

Reward rules changed:

- reward inequality;
- contract inequality;
- unresolved bidding;
- formation efficiency.

They did not reliably change completion.

Leader-priority is particularly unattractive because it increased both
coordination friction and concentration without producing a mission benefit.

## 13.7 Adaptation is not equivalent to improvement

Adaptive strategies produced many switches. Reputation values also changed
when enabled.

These state changes prove that the mechanisms operated, but not that they
improved the mission.

The distinction between mechanism activation and demonstrated benefit must be
preserved in interpretation.

## 13.8 Incentives saturate

Higher mission bonuses increased spending almost mechanically. Mission
completion did not respond with a comparable increase.

The result supports a central project conclusion:

> Incentives alone do not reliably transform decentralized competition into
> efficient mission coordination. Structural communication, capability
> compatibility, and coalition-formation capacity are more consequential under
> the tested conditions.

The experimental evidence supports:

```text
capability-heterogeneity = 0.50
communication-radius = 8
communication-failure-rate = 0.05
coalition-task-proportion = approximately 0.40 or lower
```

Reputation and adaptation may remain enabled as experimental mechanisms, but
the baseline results do not justify describing them as proven completion
enhancers.

# 17. Limitations of the experimental evidence

1. The environment is synthetic.
2. The model is dimensionless and uncalibrated.
3. Thirty seeds provide a useful repeated design but do not exhaust all
   stochastic configurations.
4. Parameter ranges were selected for mechanism comparison, not field
   estimation.
5. The experiments vary one mechanism or factorial pair at a time.
6. Higher-order interactions across communication, heterogeneity, demand,
   reward, and learning were not fully explored.
7. Coalition formation uses a greedy heuristic.
8. Reward expectations may differ from final payments.
9. Tasks do not arrive dynamically after setup.
10. No adversarial behavior or strategic defection is represented.
11. Statistical significance does not establish practical field significance.
12. The results support internal comparative conclusions, not external
    predictive validation.

# 19. Final conclusion

The experimental program demonstrates that decentralized robot coordination in
MOSAIC is governed more strongly by structural coordination capacity than by
payment strength.

The best-performing conditions combine:

- sufficient communication reach;
- moderate capability heterogeneity;
- manageable cooperative-task demand;
- a reward regime that does not create unnecessary concentration.

The principal system weakness is the ability to identify and assemble feasible
coalitions before deadlines. Stronger incentives do not correct this structural
constraint.

The model therefore supports the following release-level conclusion:

> In MOSAIC v0.4.0, self-organized mission performance depends primarily on
> information reach, capability complementarity, and coalition-formation
> capacity. Incentives shape fairness and coordination behavior, but they do not
> independently guarantee superior mission completion.
